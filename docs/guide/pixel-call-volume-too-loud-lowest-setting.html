1. Check the hearing aid setting first

Hearing aid compatibility Fixes it for some people

Android has a hearing aid compatibility mode that changes how the earpiece drives its output. It can end up enabled without you deliberately turning it on, and when it is, calls can be dramatically louder than normal at every volume level including the lowest.

This is the single most useful thing to rule out, because it costs nothing and it is instantly reversible. Where to look:

  1. Open the Phone app
  2. Tap the three line menu at the top left, then Settings
  3. Open Accessibility and find Hearing aids
  4. If it is on, turn it off. If it is already off, try turning it on and off again, which has resolved it for people whose setting had got into a stuck state
  5. Also check the system level entry: Settings, Accessibility, Hearing aids, and disable anything listed there that you are not actually using

If the call volume drops back to normal after this, you are done and nothing else on this page applies to you. If you genuinely use hearing aids and need that mode on, or if the setting was already off and nothing changed, carry on below.

2. Why calls have their own volume floor

Android keeps call audio on a separate volume scale from media. That is why turning your music down has no effect on how loud the caller sounds, and why the volume keys behave differently the moment a call connects. Each scale has its own number of steps and its own lowest step.

The problem is what that lowest step actually is. It is not a fixed loudness. It is a fixed fraction of what that particular phone can output, so on a device with a powerful earpiece, the quietest step the system offers can still be past comfortable, especially in a silent room or late at night. Nothing in the stock settings places a level underneath it.

This is also why the answer to "is my phone faulty" is usually no. When the same complaint appears about several different Pixel generations, and the hearing aid setting is not involved, the pattern points at how the scale is defined rather than at a defect on any individual handset.

3. Why the volume keys stop where they stop

Pressing volume down during a call Already at the bottom

During a call the volume keys control the call stream rather than media, and Android does not let that stream be muted with the keys. The lowest position you can reach is the system's quietest step, and holding the key longer does nothing further. If that step is too loud, the keys have no answer for you.

4. Clear Calling and adaptive sound

Clear Calling Clarity, not level

Clear Calling reduces background noise on the other person's side so their speech is easier to follow. It is a clarity feature, not a level control. Turning it on or off does not change how loud the call is in your ear, which is why people who try it specifically for this problem tend to report no difference at all.

Adaptive Sound and equalizer settings Changes tone, not level

Adaptive Sound tunes tonal balance to your surroundings, and equalizers shift energy between frequency bands. Both can take some harshness out of a voice, which is worth trying if your discomfort is about sharpness rather than sheer volume. Neither is a level control, and most equalizer features only apply to media output rather than the call path.

5. What people usually try, and what it does

The list below turns up again and again in threads about this. It is worth knowing which of these can plausibly help so you do not spend an evening on the ones that cannot.

Restarting, software updates, clearing the Phone app cache Worth one pass, rarely the answer

These are sensible first steps for any odd behaviour and they cost minutes, so run through them once. They resolve genuine glitches. They do not change where the bottom of the call volume scale sits, so if the phone has always been like this rather than suddenly changing, they are unlikely to be the fix.

Volume limiter apps and the 85 dB safety limit Caps the top, not the bottom

These exist to protect hearing from sustained loud audio. They put a ceiling on the range. If your complaint is that the quietest available level is already uncomfortable, lowering the ceiling changes nothing about where the floor sits, because you were never near the ceiling to begin with.

Bluetooth headset or speakerphone instead Different floor, same problem

Routing the call elsewhere gives you a different output path with its own minimum, which occasionally lands somewhere more comfortable. It is a workaround rather than a fix, and plenty of people find the Bluetooth minimum is too loud as well.

6. The fix: a level below the minimum

Below-floor volume reduction through Android's audio framework Actually works

Rather than trying to add steps inside the existing range, this method applies negative gain through Android's own audio effect framework. Because it reduces the level after the system has already applied its own minimum, it can place the output below where the volume keys stop.

This is what Granular Volume does for app calls such as WhatsApp: a small floating dial you can open during the call, with steps from no change down to well below your hardware minimum, as a flat reduction that makes things quieter without changing how they sound. On a regular phone call it cannot go below the lowest step your phone already offers, because Android carries cellular voice on a separate path that runs no audio effects. There the dial gives you your phone's own steps and shows the quiet steps dimmed. The mechanism is part of Android itself rather than anything Pixel specific, so it does not depend on your model or your Android version having a particular setting.

Two honest notes. It lowers the level, so use it in steps rather than going straight to the bottom, and adjust mid-call if you have gone too far. And it does not change tone, so if your real complaint is that voices sound harsh or sharp rather than loud, this addresses the loudness half of that and not the sharpness half.

FAQ

Why is my Pixel call volume too loud even at the lowest setting?
Two separate causes. The hearing aid compatibility setting raises earpiece output and can be on without you realising, so check that first. Otherwise it is the ordinary in-call floor: the lowest step is a fixed fraction of the phone's maximum output rather than a fixed loudness, and Android has no setting below it.
Where is the hearing aid setting on a Pixel?
Phone app, three line menu at the top left, Settings, Accessibility, Hearing aids. There is also a system entry under Settings, Accessibility, Hearing aids. Turning it off, or toggling it off and back on, is the first thing to try.
Does Clear Calling reduce call volume?
No. It reduces background noise on the other person's side to make speech clearer. It changes clarity rather than level, so it does not make the call quieter in your ear.
Why do the volume keys stop before it is quiet enough?
During a call the keys control the call stream, which Android does not allow to be muted with the keys. The lowest position is the quietest step the system offers, and there is nothing below it in stock settings.
Is my Pixel faulty, or does it need a repair?
Usually neither. If the hearing aid setting is off and the earpiece still feels too loud at the minimum, the phone is behaving as designed. The same complaint recurs across several Pixel generations in Google's own support community, which points at how the volume scale is defined rather than a defect on one unit.
What actually adds a quieter level below the minimum?
A volume cut applied through Android's own audio effect framework, after the system has already applied its own minimum. It reaches media, ringtones, notifications, alarms and app calls such as WhatsApp. It does not reach regular phone calls: Android carries cellular voice on a path that runs no audio effects, so no app can lower it further.
Does it need root, a computer, or a Google account?
No. It uses Android's own audio-effect framework, needs only a display-over-other-apps permission and standard audio permissions, and requires no root, ADB, or computer.
