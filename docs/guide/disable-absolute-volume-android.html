How to disable absolute volume on Android

The exact path on Pixel, Samsung, Xiaomi and OnePlus, including where it moved in Android 17, what the switch really changes for Bluetooth earbuds and headphones, the one thing that quietly turns it back on, and what to do when the lowest step is still too loud.

Updated October 29, 2026 · 7 min read

Disable absolute volume is the setting people are pointed to when Bluetooth earbuds or headphones are too loud even on the lowest step, or when the phone's volume and the headset's volume seem to fight each other. It is hidden in Developer options, its name reads backwards, and it can switch itself back off without telling you. Here is where it is, what it does, and what it cannot do.

Short answer

Turn on Developer options by tapping Build number seven times, open Developer options, and switch on Disable absolute volume. Then turn Bluetooth off and on so the headset reconnects. Leave Developer options on: switching them off resets this setting. It separates the phone's volume from the headset's own volume; it does not add a level quieter than the phone's lowest step. What absolute volume is

With absolute volume on, which is the default, the phone does not make Bluetooth audio quieter by itself. It sends the audio at full level and tells the headset which volume to play at, so the phone's slider and the headset's own volume are one shared control. When a headset maps those levels well, that works nicely. When it maps them badly, the lowest step lands too loud, the steps jump, or the two volumes disagree.

That is exactly the case the switch exists for. Android's own description says it disables the feature “in case of volume issues with remote devices such as unacceptably loud volume”.

How to turn it off, step by step

1. Turn on Developer options

Find Build number and tap it seven times, then enter your PIN if asked. A message tells you that you are now a developer. Where Build number sits depends on the phone:

Google Pixel and most phones close to stock AndroidSettings, About phone, Build numberSettings, System, Developer options
Samsung GalaxySettings, About phone, Software information, Build numberThe bottom of the main Settings list
Xiaomi, Redmi, POCO (HyperOS)Settings, About phone, Detailed info and specs, OS version (MIUI version on older phones)Settings, Additional settings, Developer options
OnePlusSettings, About device, Version, Build numberSettings, System settings or Additional settings, Developer options

Menu names shift between versions and models. If you cannot find an entry, type build number into the Settings search.

Brand guides cover the whole too-loud problem, not only this switch: Samsung Galaxy, Xiaomi, Redmi and POCO, and OnePlus.

2. Switch on Disable absolute volume

Open Developer options and look for Disable absolute volume. Up to Android 16, on Pixel and phones close to stock Android, it is in the Networking section, among the Bluetooth entries. On Android 17 it moved: Developer options now has its own Bluetooth page, and the switch is inside it, on the same page as the Bluetooth codec settings. On any phone, typing absolute into the Settings search usually finds it.

Mind the wording: the switch is named after what it turns off. Switch on means absolute volume is off.

3. Reconnect the headset

Turn Bluetooth off and on, or disconnect and reconnect the earbuds, then set the volume again. If nothing seems different, restart the phone once.

4. Leave Developer options on

This is the step most guides skip. In Android's Settings code, switching Developer options off also resets Disable absolute volume to off. If you tidy up afterwards by turning Developer options off, you undo the change.

Missing on Android 17?

It moved rather than disappeared. In Android 17's own Settings code, the switch left the main Developer options list and now sits on a new Bluetooth page inside Developer options, still named Disable absolute volume. Open Developer options, tap Bluetooth, and switch it on there, or search Settings for absolute. Leaving Developer options on still matters on this version.

Some people testing later Android 17 beta updates on Pixel report that the switch is not there at all, and we have not been able to check those builds. If your phone has no such switch, two things are left: try a different Bluetooth audio codec on the same Bluetooth page, and to get below the lowest step, lower the sound on the phone before it reaches the headset, which is what the dial further down this page does.

What changes afterwards

Now there are two volumes: the phone's slider scales the audio, and the headset keeps its own level. Which way that goes depends on the headset, not on the phone.

Finer, quieter low steps When it helps

On some headsets the low end becomes usable: the bottom steps sit lower and closer together, and the volume stops jumping.

Louder than before When it backfires

Some headsets sit at their own full level once they no longer follow the phone, so every step is louder. Lower the headset's own volume with its buttons or its companion app, or switch the setting back off.

The lowest step is still too loud Not something this switch can fix

Absolute volume decides how the level is shared between phone and headset. It does not add a level below the phone's lowest step. If the quietest step is still too loud, read on.

To undo everything, switch Disable absolute volume off again and reconnect the headset.

Why it turns itself back on

If the setting keeps reverting, check three things. Developer options were switched off, which resets it, as above. A system update may have reset Developer options. On Xiaomi phones, Xiaomi notes that running the Security app's optimisation can hide the Developer options entry, so open it again from Additional settings and check the switch.

FAQ

Where is the Disable absolute volume setting on Android?
In Developer options. Turn those on by tapping Build number seven times (on Samsung under About phone, Software information; on Xiaomi tap OS version under About phone, Detailed info and specs), then open Developer options and switch on Disable absolute volume. On Pixel and most phones close to stock Android it sits in the Networking section up to Android 16; on Android 17 it is on the new Bluetooth page inside Developer options. Typing "absolute" into the Settings search finds it on most phones.
Disable absolute volume is missing on Android 17. Was it removed?
In Android 17 it moved. Android's own Settings code for Android 17 places it on a new Bluetooth page inside Developer options instead of the main list. Search Settings for "absolute" to jump straight to it. Some people on later Android 17 betas for Pixel report it missing entirely; if yours has none, try a different Bluetooth codec on that page, and for the lowest step use a dial that lowers the sound on the phone.
Does disabling absolute volume make earbuds quieter?
Sometimes, not always. It splits the phone's volume from the headset's own volume. On some headsets that gives quieter, finer low steps. On others the headset stays at its own full level and the result is louder, in which case lower the headset's own volume with its buttons or companion app, or switch the setting back off. It never adds a level below the phone's lowest step.
Why did absolute volume turn itself back on?
Most often because Developer options were switched off. In Android's own Settings code, turning Developer options off resets Disable absolute volume to off. Leave Developer options on if you want the setting to stay. On Xiaomi phones, Xiaomi notes that running the Security app's optimisation can hide the Developer options entry.
Do I need to restart or reconnect after changing it?
Reconnect the headset: turn Bluetooth off and on, or disconnect and reconnect the earbuds, then set the volume again. If nothing seems to change, restart the phone once.
Is it safe to change?
Yes. It is a standard Android setting that you can switch back at any time. Developer options hold other settings too; change only this one and leave the rest as they are.
My earbuds are still too loud at the lowest step. What now?
Absolute volume decides how the level is shared between phone and headset; it does not move the floor. To go below the lowest step, Quiet Dial adds six quieter steps, 5 dB each, down to 30 dB under the hardware minimum, by lowering Android's own output. It works with absolute volume on or off. On a few phone and headset pairs the quiet steps have no effect over Bluetooth, so check yours during the seven free days.
