Quiet white noise for a sleeping baby, when the minimum volume is still too loud

Phones and tablets are built to be heard across a room. A crib a few feet away needs something much quieter than that, and Android's own volume slider usually can't get there.

Updated October 4, 2026 · 5 min read

White noise and lullaby apps are one of the most common reasons people go looking for a quieter volume setting than their phone offers. The problem isn't the app playing the sound. It's that the device's lowest non-mute volume step is calibrated for normal listening, not for something meant to be barely audible next to a sleeping baby.

Android's volume system divides the range between silence and full output into a fixed number of steps, and the first step above mute is a fixed fraction of the device's maximum volume, not a fixed loudness. A phone or tablet with a louder maximum ends up with a louder floor too, even though every device shows the same "step 1 of however many" on the slider. This is exactly why the same white-noise app can sound fine at minimum volume on one phone and be uncomfortably loud on another, or on a tablet versus a phone from the same person's own house.

The short answer

Android's lowest volume step is a fixed fraction of the phone's maximum, so on many phones and tablets it is still too loud for a crib a few feet away, and no setting moves it. Moving the phone farther away helps, and some white-noise apps have their own volume slider. Why turning the volume all the way down still isn't quiet enough

It's tempting to assume any remaining loudness at the lowest step is a hardware limitation that can't be worked around. It isn't. It's a software limitation, specifically the fact that stock Android has no control for anything quieter than step 1. The fix has to live outside the volume slider itself, cutting the volume underneath the existing floor rather than trying to subdivide it further.

Apps that add "more precise" volume steps (100 clicks instead of 15, for example) help a few dB and then stop, because Android's loudness curve has a fixed bottom that no step count passes.

What actually works for this specific case

An app that applies negative gain through Android's own audio effect framework, rather than remapping the existing volume steps, genuinely produces output quieter than the hardware floor. FAQ

Will white noise glitch or cut out at the lowest level?
No. The reduction is a continuous gain stage, not a mute or a compression effect, so a white noise loop plays exactly as smoothly at -30 dB as it does at 0 dB, just quieter throughout.
Can I leave it running at a fixed quiet level all night?
Yes. The dial's level is saved and stays put until you change it. Set it once to the level that works, then minimize the dial to a small tab at the edge of the screen. It lowers media, alarms, ringtones and notifications together, so if the same phone has to wake you or ring for you, check that you can still hear it at that step.
Does it change the sound quality of the white noise?
It applies flat gain reduction, not an equalizer curve, so it doesn't reshape the frequency content the way turning down a bass-heavy EQ band would. White noise sounds the same, just quieter.
