## What this means

(1) Strip "only app" language from every asset now — Play listing, XDA thread, site, videos, VO scripts. It is refutable by a screenshot of a rival's dev site. Replace with the narrowed, testable claims: purpose-built, one tap, no root, no ADB, no Shizuku, works on every app including Spotify and Chrome, free, no ads, down to -30dB. (2) Premise-first rule applies before any further marketing effort: run the ~2-hour device/emulator test all four reports converged on — measure VC:LoB's Ultra-Low mode, Precise Volume's post-gain floor, Wavelet's gain range, SoundAssistant at step 1 on a Samsung. Every outcome pays: either we gain a truthful measured-superiority claim ("goes to -30dB where the 5M-install incumbent stops at -18dB", "the one that actually drops below the floor") or we reposition honestly before spending on a broken premise — the same premise class that burned us on demand. (3) The Sep 1 gate and its thin-trickle stranger-only KPIs stand unchanged: this audit does not upgrade demand, it clarifies that the thin trickle is partially intercepted upstream (SEO fix articles, VC:LoB owning the generic shelf) while we own the exact-phrase shelf and F-Droid. Judge the 5-week test against the thin-trickle baseline; weight store-search impressions and stranger installs over forum-thread counts, because verified SEO interception means the pain now shows up as searches, not posts. (4) Decision posture: the uniqueness premise is DOWNGRADED from "only solution" to "cleanest solution in a contested niche". That still justifies running the test to Sep 1 at near-zero cost, but it removes any justification for pre-gate investment that assumes uniqueness-driven organic pull, and it makes the device-test homework a prerequisite for the comparative copy the test would otherwise run on.

## Appendix: fact-checked lens summaries

### Why did "lowest volume too loud" complaints decline (2011-2020 peak vs near-zero flow today)? Solved, reduced, or dispersed? [FACT-CHECKED]

Fact-check result: the report's three most load-bearing claims all survived independent reproduction. I re-fetched (1) the F-Droid forum request thread — OP dated February 20, 2024, complaining "the lowest volume level on my phone is still too loud, especially when using earphones", with only Volume Control / Hearing Saver / VolumeLockr suggested, none claiming sub-minimum attenuation; (2) the GSMArena article — published May 29, 2017, confirming SoundAssistant's 150 volume steps and Galaxy S8/S8+ availability; (3) the Android Central thread — OP Aug 4, 2022 (Galaxy S22+), solved via the SoundAssistant/Good Lock recommendation, OP replied "Working perfect", and an April 10, 2024 reply called it a game changer. The core verdict REDUCED + DISPERSED, not SOLVED, stands.

However, the report's implication that the residual below-the-floor niche is "unserved" is overstated, and the lens MISSED two material absorption channels. First, the third-party Play Store ecosystem: Precise Volume 2.0 + Equalizer (com.phascinate.precisevolume) has an estimated 1,000,000+ downloads and explicitly "overrides Android's default 15-25 volume steps and allows you to set a fully custom number" — a decade-old, million-install commercial product sitting on exactly this pain. It is not identical to true sub-minimum attenuation, but users combine its 1% scaling with EQ post-gain reduction to get below the stock floor, and it is the standard recommendation for non-Samsung devices. Any "we serve an unserved residue" framing must be narrowed to "unserved by platform/OEM, contested by at least one large third-party app." Second, SEO interception: evergreen how-to content (e.g., mobileinternist's "Phone volume too loud on the lowest setting? Top 6 fixes", published Dec 2020 and actively maintained — updated Jan 12, 2025) now catches searchers and hands them the workarounds (SoundAssistant, Precise Volume, disabling Bluetooth Absolute Volume) before they ever post a new forum thread. This is a fourth dispersal mechanism the lens missed: complaint flow declined partly because the question is now answered at the search-results layer, so the pain generates page views instead of threads. An actively-updated 2025 fix article is itself evidence the pain still has enough search volume to be worth maintaining content for.

Verification hygiene: several claims the original report marked verified rested on search-engine snippets the reporting lens never opened (Xiaomi MIUI 12, EN 50332, Bluetooth absolute volume intro date, Cirrus Logic amps, AOSP-never-fixed). Under the stingy standard — verified means the fact-checker reproduced it — I downgraded those to unverified/plausible; none was contradicted, and I directly corroborated one piece of the Bluetooth claim (disabling Absolute Volume is a documented standard fix for this exact pain, per the fetched mobileinternist article). The Samsung Community 403-blocked threads remain correctly marked unverified, though my own independent search re-surfaced the same S24 FE snippet ("either the volume is too low to hear anything, or the next step up is already too loud"), so they are snippet-corroborated from two independent search runs.

### play-sweep (fact-checked)

The report's core verdict survives fact-checking: uniqueness holds at the POSITIONING level (no Play app is built for or markets below-minimum volume), but not at the strict CAPABILITY level. However, the capability gap is WIDER than the report says. The report treats Precise Volume's EQ post-gain as a single back door; independent searching shows it is a category pattern. Wavelet (com.pittvandewitt.wavelet, 1M+ installs on Play, no root), which the report never mentions, has developer-documented gain controls — a limiter post-gain plus a channel-balance feature the docs explicitly say "you can also use... to control the overall volume output" — with the same session-detection/legacy-mode architecture as Precise Volume. Any session-attaching EQ with negative gain (Wavelet, Precise Volume, likely Poweramp Equalizer) can attenuate below the lowest system step on supported devices. Marketing language must therefore be "the only app BUILT FOR going below minimum volume — one tap, no EQ digging, no ADB", never "the only app that CAN".

On the specific claims: the DUMP-permission/ADB/experimental limitation reproduced verbatim from the developer's own docs, and the F-Droid forum thread reproduced by direct fetch (no responder claimed any recommended app goes below minimum). Precise Volume's scale reproduced via AppBrain: 5,000,000+ downloads, updated Nov 11 2025, 4.34 stars on ~32K ratings — slightly different numbers than the report's "4.0 stars, 34.2K reviews, Nov 12" but the load-bearing substance (live, major, actively updated) stands. The claimed -18 dB post-gain floor remains UNVERIFIED after multiple targeted searches: post-gain exists and users do turn it down for quieter-than-step output (XDA thread evidence), but no source I could reach states a -18 dB floor or negative-value support explicitly; only an emulator install settles it. The Volume Control: Lower or Boost review quotes (Zenny/Jen T) could not be reproduced and are downgraded to unverified; the listing's lower-volume positioning is real. Samsung Sound Assistant's mechanism reproduced (150-increment media scale, step slider 1-10, step=1 gives the finest granularity per Gadget Hacks), and its US Play listing 404s, supporting "Galaxy Store, not Play" — though MakeUseOf claims it is on both stores, so regional/device-gated Play availability cannot be ruled out.

### foss-tech (fact-checked)

The core of the report survives independent verification. I reproduced, from primary sources, the three most load-bearing claims: (1) RootlessJamesDSP's output postgain really is -15 to +15 dB with limiter threshold down to -60 dB (raw preferences XML fetched directly from the repo); (2) its README really names Spotify, Google Chrome, and SoundCloud as unprocessed/unsupported due to capture blocking, and lists increased audio latency; (3) the F-Droid listing really requires 'Shizuku (Android 11+) or ADB access via a computer' for initial setup. I also reproduced the ADB limitation (error text 'invalid volume index 20 for stream 3 (should be in [0..15])' confirms integer-index-only) and the F-Droid catalog sweep (20 'volume' results, none besides Granular Volume addressing loud-at-minimum). The lens's verdict — no FOSS/technical alternative satisfies all four criteria — stands on these points.

However, per the stingy-verification rule, I downgraded every claim I did not personally reproduce (Wavelet post-gain docs, root modules, Poweramp/Neutron, Sound Amplifier, Precise Volume v1/XDA) to verified:false with the original evidence retained; they are plausible but were not re-fetched in this pass. More importantly, the lens UNDERWEIGHTED two threats. First, Samsung Sound Assistant: the lens said there was 'no discussion of enabling sub-minimum levels,' but a how-to guide (mobileinternist.com) explicitly claims Sound Assistant 'can override system settings and lower volume even more,' letting users set 1% instead of a 10% floor via 'Change step volume.' Whether 1% is genuinely below the stock hardware step is still untested, but published guidance directly markets it as a below-minimum fix on Samsung — roughly half the Android installed base in many markets. Second, Precise Volume: community guidance (its docs mention post-gain as 'Volume Booster' under Graphic EQ, and search-surfaced user reports describe using negative post-gain to get below ~5% for sensitive Bluetooth/bone-conduction headphones) shows the below-minimum use-case is already circulating among its users, even though the official docs page I fetched contains no explicit below-minimum documentation, no range, and a 'Careful with this!' warning. It remains UNVERIFIED mechanically but should be treated as the #1 competitive unknown, ahead of where the lens placed it.

New finds the lens missed entirely: (a) RootlessJamesDSP's F-Droid listing states it 'Cannot coexist with (some) other audio effect apps (e.g., Wavelet and other apps that make use of the DynamicsProcessing Android API)' — meaning RJDSP and DynamicsProcessing-based apps (including ours) conflict on-device; users must choose one, which cuts both ways competitively. (b) The only documented system-level route to a lower floor is editing /vendor/etc/audio_policy_volumes.xml volume curves — root-only and wiped on every OTA (veronneau.org) — which strengthens the no-root moat claim.
